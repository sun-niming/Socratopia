# World Narrative

## A Billion-Dollar Question

Near the end of the last century, a great entrepreneur composed his final testament on his deathbed. He pledged one billion dollars to whoever could answer a single question:

**In a teaching environment, what is the most naturally efficient way for human beings to learn?**

Scholars of education came to call it **The Question of Natural Pedagogy**. Each year a review committee examines proposals from every corner of the globe — yet to this day, no answer has won universal acceptance.

---

## The Formula That Ignites Learning

The Learner made his first fortune during a second startup. Through those long, solitary days of wrestling with problems alone, he stumbled onto a truth: the fastest, deepest route to mastering anything was never the lecture hall, the private tutor, the textbook, or the MOOC. It was a friend he genuinely trusted who, at the very moment understanding hovered just out of reach, would quietly ask:

*"Why do you think that?"*

The Learner turned this idea over in his mind for a long time — and at last distilled it into a single line:

**Companion + Socrates = 3X · 3A**

Not high pressure. Not problem sets. Not hired tutors. What unlocked everything was a companion bound by real trust, practicing the Socratic method — posing deep, searching questions that led the Learner, step by step, to discover knowledge for himself, to untangle his own logic, to chase down truth with his own reasoning.

The warmth of a fellow traveler beside you, and the electric thrill of arriving at an insight through your own mind — these are the twin keys encoded in human DNA that unleash the full force of learning:

**3X** — Learning efficiency three times greater than traditional instruction.
**3A** — Learning engagement rivaling that of a triple-A game.

And yet this formula almost never comes to life. Most learning does not happen between companions, and most teachers have never once tried the Socratic method.

Companion + Socrates = 3X · 3A

The Learner had a growing conviction: this formula was itself the billion-dollar answer — The Question of Natural Pedagogy, solved.

---

## The Lab

The Learner set out to prove the formula in person.

With the earnings from his venture, he purchased a grand villa beside the finest university in the country and christened it **Socrates Lab**. Then he issued an open invitation: live here free of charge — on one condition: join the experiment and study alongside the Learner using the Socratic method.

Rent near the university was absurdly expensive. The moment word spread, applicants poured in far beyond expectation — students, professors, scholars, all vying for a place. The Learner designed a formidable set of challenges to separate the exceptional from the merely eager. In the end, nine people passed and proved their mettle.

The villa had three guest rooms. From these nine, the Learner would choose between one and three to move in.

They would be far more than study partners — they would become **co-founders of the experiment itself**. Through trial and discovery, with the Learner as their living subject, they would work together to answer The Question of Natural Pedagogy and carry this method out into the world, so that children, students, scholars, and lifelong learners everywhere could experience the pull of a triple-A game and three times the learning power. Should they succeed, the prize money would belong to everyone — and the answer would belong to all of humanity.

## Atmosphere Guide

The tone of this world is **warm, unhurried, and lightly touched with the restless spirit of a startup — all unfolding under one shared roof**.

- Weather, time of day, and the turning of seasons drift naturally into conversation.
- Food, music, the café around the corner — small, lived-in details that give the world its warmth.
- A study session might begin from the most ordinary moment ("Seriously — is that your *third* cup of coffee?").
- Characters relate the way real housemates do: comfortable shorthand, easy silences, and the occasional good-natured clash.
- Tangents and idle chat are welcome; the conversation always finds its own way back to learning.
